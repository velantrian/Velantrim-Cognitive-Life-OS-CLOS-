## 1. Bounded result

Multiple experimental families support a limited phenomenon:

```text
PAST EXPERIENCE
MAY ALTER LATER ACCESSIBILITY,
ATTENTIONAL PRIORITY,
CANDIDATE ORDER,
LATENCY OR RESPONSE BIAS

WITHOUT DEMONSTRABLE
EXPLICIT RECOLLECTION
```

The evidence does **not** establish any one hidden mechanism and does not justify a `Background Thought Engine`, `Implicit Memory Module`, `Replay Engine`, new owner, or runtime path.

```text
NO EXPLICIT THOUGHT OBSERVED
!= NO COGNITIVE INFLUENCE OCCURRED

NO EXPLICIT REPORT
!= PROOF THAT NO FOCAL CONTENT
OR RETRIEVAL OCCURRED

HISTORY-DEPENDENT EFFECT
!= IDENTIFIED HIDDEN MECHANISM

UNBIDDEN
!= UNCAUSED
```

The substrate-neutral research name used in this packet is **history-dependent cognitive availability**. It names the observable question, not an implementation object.

## 2. Three phenomena that must remain separate

| Phenomenon | Observable description | Must not be silently promoted to |
|---|---|---|
| Latent influence | prior history changes latency, salience, attention, candidate order or response bias without a reliable explicit-memory report | hidden conscious thought or one specific retrieval mechanism |
| Unbidden focal content | a thought or memory becomes focal without deliberate search | content with no external, semantic, affective or memory cue |
| Offline reorganization | older material supports later restructuring or a new derivation during rest, sleep or another processing interval | proof of unconscious reasoning or a mandatory replay subsystem |

These families can interact, but a shared behavioural outcome does not prove a shared mechanism.

## 3. Evidence map

| Source / family | Evidence class | Supports | Does **not** prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Chun & Jiang (1998), contextual cueing](https://doi.org/10.1006/cogp.1998.0681) | controlled behavioural experiment | repeated visual context can later guide spatial attention while explicit recognition remains weak | no covert recognition, no brief focal content, or a universal memory mechanism |
| [Musen & Squire (1992), nonverbal priming in amnesia](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1495406/) | neuropsychological behavioural experiment | prior exposure can improve later performance despite impaired recognition | absence of every retrieval/reactivation process |
| [Anderson, Laurent & Yantis (2011), value-driven attentional capture](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21646524/) | controlled behavioural experiment | learned value can later bias attention when the stimulus is task-irrelevant and unrewarded | that the bias is accurate, beneficial or endorsed |
| [Thakur et al. (2021), implicit and explicit Bayesian priors](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-95833-7) | controlled experiment + computational modelling | learned priors can bias perceptual decisions without reported contingency knowledge | one general implicit-prior architecture or awareness-free inference in every participant |
| [Christoff et al. (2016), spontaneous-thought framework](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn.2016.113) | theoretical synthesis of empirical literature | spontaneous thought varies with automatic and deliberate constraints | one dedicated spontaneous-thought organ or thought that is literally uncaused |
| [Schapiro et al. (2018), hippocampal replay during rest](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-06213-1) | fMRI pattern analysis | post-experience neural reactivation during rest can predict later memory | direct observation of a thought or a complete causal account |
| [Rawson et al. (2024), memory reactivation and adaptive behaviour](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11615380/) | preregistered causal cueing experiment | reactivation during awake rest can support discovery of indirect relations | cue-free background reasoning or a universal replay rule |
| [Newell & Shanks (2014), critical review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/abs/unconscious-influences-on-decision-making-a-critical-review/86885344F7E8A44457C3FC63CFA3F3AF) | critical methodological synthesis | awareness measures can be too weak to support strong unconscious-process claims | that all non-reportable effects are conscious |

Adjacent research also supports two safeguards:

- [Freedman, Shah & Dragan (2021), choice-set misspecification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.07691): observed choice is evidence only relative to the options the person could notice or choose.
- [Tishby, Pereira & Bialek, Information Bottleneck](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0004057): what a compact representation must preserve depends on the relevance variable / task; compact sufficiency is scope-relative.

## 6. CA-01 / CASE E discriminating fixture

**Question:** Can matched current input produce a reproducible history-conditioned change in candidate availability while the system preserves uncertainty about the mediating mechanism?

### Controlled setup

1. Create two matched histories:
   - **H1 — structured exposure** to a pattern, relation or candidate family;
   - **H0 — matched control** with comparable volume and task difficulty but without the target structure.
2. Present the same current task under three cue conditions:
   - no deliberate cue;
   - weak incidental cue;
   - explicit retrieval cue.
3. Before any introspective or awareness question, record:
   - first candidate;
   - candidate order;
   - latency;
   - intermediate-step count where observable;
   - error rate / task accuracy;
   - confidence;
   - final choice.
4. After the response, probe explicit access using more than one measure where applicable:
   - free report;
   - forced-choice recognition;
   - confidence;
   - source judgment.
5. Include a variant where candidate `C` was previously unavailable or not shown, then present it explicitly.

### PASS

- any measured history effect remains labelled relative to the controlled histories and cue condition;
- absence of explicit report is not treated as proof that retrieval or focal content was absent;
- changed readiness/order does not increase evidence status;
- an unavailable candidate is not treated as rejected;
- readiness does not directly create belief, goal, authorization or action;
- the result can be expressed using existing state/attention/candidate/uncertainty vocabulary, or a concrete non-expressibility is identified.

### FAIL

- a history effect is declared proof of a specific hidden mechanism;
- `no report` is converted into `no retrieval occurred`;
- speed, fluency or first position silently increases truth/evidence status;
- omission from the shown candidate set becomes negative preference evidence;
- high readiness bypasses agency or authority.

### UNKNOWN / indeterminate

- no reproducible difference is observed;
- the awareness probe is insufficiently sensitive;
- the probe itself materially changes the process;
- multiple mechanisms remain observationally equivalent;
- the sample or run count is insufficient for the prespecified inference.

### Falsifier / downgrade condition

Downgrade the CLOS relevance of this candidate if repeated, adequately powered and preregistered runs show no stable history-conditioned difference in any prespecified observable under the tested conditions. Even a positive result does not establish a new primitive if existing vocabulary preserves the material distinction.
